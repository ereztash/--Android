# M11 — real-word errors, found by context

**Requirement, from the operator:** *"אפליקציה צריכה לזהות גם בתוך משפט לפי ההקשר אם יש שגיאת
כתיב כמו שימוש באם במקום עם"* — the app must detect, inside a sentence and from context, a
spelling error such as using `אם` where `עם` was meant.

## Plan (written before the work, per §0)

| # | Step | How it is kept honest |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confusion sets from Hebrew **phonology**, not from the corpus. | Derived from which letters share a realisation, so the inventory is not fitted to the data it is scored on. |
| 2 | Score with the M10 bigram table: does the corpus support the alternative in this context? | Reuses a table already built and gated. |
| 3 | Cut a **dev** and a **test** slice, proven disjoint from each other and from M10's. | `slice_eval_corpus.py` compares index sets and refuses to write on any overlap. |
| 4 | Sweep on dev only. | Thresholds are chosen once, on sentences the reported numbers never touch. |
| 5 | Report once on test, with recall and false alarms in the same assertion. | Recall alone is meaningless — a detector that flags everything has perfect recall. |
| 6 | Never auto-apply. | Structural: the detector returns a suggestion and the service has no auto-replace path for it. |

## What shipped

- `HebrewConfusions` — six homophone letter pairs, variants generated on demand against the
  lexicon. **Zero asset bytes**; the full inventory would have been megabytes.
- `RealWordErrorDetector` — offers an alternative only when the corpus supports it *and* has
  nothing to say for what was typed.
- `TypingContext` and `InputContextBuffer.completedWords` / `tailFromCompletedWord` — enough
  context to check the middle of a three-word window, and the span arithmetic to fix a word
  that is not under the cursor.
- The strip shows `עם (אם)` for these, naming the word it would replace, and TalkBack says
  "replaces … earlier in the sentence".

Measured: **64.58% recall at a 0.26% false-alarm rate** on a test slice the thresholds never
saw. All numbers, controls and limits:
[`docs/CONFUSION_MEASUREMENTS.md`](../CONFUSION_MEASUREMENTS.md).

## The harness bug, and what it nearly cost

The first sweep reported that using only left context cost **1.05 points** of recall. On that
basis the architecture was settled: check each word as it is finished, never touch text away
from the cursor, and skip the span arithmetic entirely.

The harness was wrong. Its "left only" branch still passed the following word to the detector;
only the set of eligible positions varied. Corrected, the real gap is **19.64 points** — 44.78%
against 64.42% — and it reverses the decision.

It surfaced because the locked test reported 44.69% against a floor of 60.0 written from the
dev sweep's 63.37%. A 19-point gap between two disjoint slices of one corpus is not sampling
noise. The lesson is the same one M9 taught with the mirrored keyboard: **a harness that
measures the wrong thing agrees with itself perfectly.** What caught it was not a review of the
harness but a number that disagreed with another number, which is why the dev/test split earns
its keep beyond the usual argument about overfitting.

Cost recorded: the discrepancy took one extra look at the test slice. No threshold moved as a
result — the harness was fixed and the architecture decision reversed.

## What M11 does NOT do

- **No claim about real-world error rates.** Recall is measured against injected errors drawn
  from the detector's own inventory. How often genuine Hebrew typing produces an error *inside*
  that inventory is NOT MEASURED, and would need a corpus of real human errors.
- **No grammar.** The evidence is two words wide. Agreement, tense, and anything needing
  sentence structure are invisible.
- **No `ו`/`י`.** The largest pair by count and mostly not a confusion at all — `ktiv male`
  alternation makes those two genuinely contrastive. Available and unmeasured.
- **No keyboard-adjacency pairs.** A different error model with a separately measured rate;
  merging them would blur both.
- **Never auto-applies.** Rewriting `אם` to `עם` without being asked would change what someone
  said into something they did not say.
